UK home insurance risk map

2,736 postcode districts · subsidence × weather × flood × groundwater · C-vine copula
Methodology

Perils — real open data. The subsidence score is BGS 625k bedrock geology classified for shrink–swell susceptibility (London Clay/Thames Group, Gault, Oxford & Kimmeridge Clays, Mercia Mudstone…; pre-Mesozoic mudstones down-weighted), area-weighted over each district. The weather score blends Met Office grids interpolated to each district: winter mean wind speed (5 km), the annual wind-driven rain index for SW-facing walls (5 km), annual count of ≥10 mm rain days and annual precipitation (HadUK-Grid observations 1991–2020).

Marginals. Annual aggregate loss per policy per peril is compound Bernoulli × LogNormal, with claim frequency and severity driven by the scores.

Flood. Official flood extents rasterised per district: rivers & sea from EA NaFRA2 present-day defended extents (England), NRW FRAW (Wales) and SEPA maps (Scotland) at 100 m; surface water from EA RoFSW (13 m, category colours decoded), NRW FRAW surface water and SEPA surface water maps. Zone fractions drive the flood claim frequency directly; surface-water claims are modelled shallower and cheaper than river/sea.

Groundwater. EA groundwater flood alert target areas via the postcode search tool data: per district, the share of unit postcodes flagged ‘Possible’. England only (UK groundwater flooding concentrates on the English chalk); a nominal background elsewhere.

Coastal erosion. EA NCERM National 2024 frontages: the strip of each district projected to be lost to the sea by 2105, under the adopted Shoreline Management Plan and under no-further-intervention. Not part of the premium — standard household policies exclude gradual erosion — so it is shown as blight and valuation exposure only. England only.

Vine copula. The five perils are joined by a C-vine with weather at the root. Tree 1: Gumbel(θWF up to 3.0) weather–flood, Gumbel(θWG up to 2.4) weather–groundwater (aquifer recharge follows prolonged rain), Gumbel(θWS up to 2.5) weather–subsidence, Gumbel(θWE up to 2.6) weather–erosion (coasts retreat in storms) — all upper-tail dependent, so extreme years hit perils together. Tree 2 (given weather): Gaussian ρ=0.35 flood–erosion (the same surges drive both, making this the strongest of the three), ρ=0.25 flood–groundwater, ρ=0.15 flood–subsidence; tree 3 independent. 20,000 simulated years per district (Marshall–Olkin for the root pair, conditional h-function inversion for the other margins). A tau-matched 5-dim Gaussian copula and an independence run are computed for comparison. There is deliberately no per-district copula uplift layer: at calibrated frequencies that difference is inside Monte Carlo noise, so it would be drawing noise across the country. The dependence effect is real, but it lives in the portfolio.

Calibration. Each peril's claim frequency is scaled to the ABI's published 2025 domestic payouts — storm £244m, flood £312m, subsidence £307m over ~15.5m policies — and severities take the published average claim as their mean. Together the four insured perils come to ~£56/policy/yr, about a quarter of what all home claims cost. Coastal erosion is not in that figure and is not calibrated to ABI at all — it has no published payout, because it is not covered. These are four-peril premiums, not home insurance premiums: a real policy also covers escape of water, theft, fire and accidental damage, plus expenses and profit. What is not fitted is the geography and the dependence.

Rating groups. Technical premium = expected loss + 6% cost-of-capital on the district's Euler-allocated share of portfolio tail risk — its expected loss given the whole book is in its worst 1% of years — so cross-district diversification is credited rather than charging every home for its own private worst year. TVaR (expected shortfall) rather than VaR: with rare compound losses, tail dependence concentrates claims into fewer, worse years, so VaR can move the wrong way while TVaR stays monotone in dependence.

Hazard data: BGS Geology 625k, Met Office HadUK-Grid / Climate Data Portal, EA / NRW / SEPA flood maps, EA flood-risk postcode data (all OGL). Loss frequencies/severities and copula parameters are illustrative assumptions, not calibrated to claims. Boundaries: uk-postcode-polygons.
