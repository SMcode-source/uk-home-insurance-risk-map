What the model says

Four findings that fall out of the simulation.

+57%catastrophic-year cost

Ignoring dependence understates the tail badly

In the worst 1% of years the portfolio costs £15,460 per policy under the vine copula, against £9,831 if the perils were treated as independent. Storms, floods and shrink–swell droughts are driven by the same climate; modelling them separately quietly removes the years that hurt.

×9flood cost, typical → bad

Bad years aren't just "more weather"

Weather is the workhorse loss in every year, but it is the secondary perils that turn a typical year (£262 per policy) into a bad one (£2,400). Going from typical to bad, flood cost multiplies 9× and subsidence 11×, while weather multiplies just 8×.

TVaRnot VaR

The obvious risk measure gives the wrong answer here

With rare compound losses, tail dependence concentrates claims into fewer, worse years — pushing losses beyond the 99.5% quantile. Value-at-Risk can therefore fall as dependence rises. Premiums use expected shortfall (TVaR 99%), which is monotone in dependence and doesn't hide the clustering.

Every hazard input is public

BGS geology, Met Office climatologies, Environment Agency, Natural Resources Wales and SEPA flood maps, EA groundwater alert areas — all Open Government Licence, all fetched by scripts in the repo. Nothing here depends on a licensed hazard product.
