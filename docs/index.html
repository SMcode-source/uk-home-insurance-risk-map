A UK home-insurance risk model that scores every postcode district for subsidence, weather damage, flood and groundwater flooding — then joins them with a vine copula, because the expensive years are the ones where several perils strike at once. Coastal erosion is modelled alongside them but kept out of the premium, because no standard policy covers it.

Separately, 352 districts sit on a coast the sea is projected to take land from by 2105. For 80 of them the adopted defence plans hold that at zero — the clearest thing in the model that sea walls do. Worst case is HU19, losing 28% of its area even with its plan in place. None of this is in the premium: no standard policy covers coastal erosion.

And re-running the whole model on the Environment Agency's climate-change flood extents — calibration held fixed, so only the hazard map moves — shifts the technical premium by +10% across the 2,087 districts it covers, worst DN32 at +256%. What the model says

Four findings that fall out of the simulation.

×94multi-peril years

Dependence changes what can happen, not what usually does

Storms, floods and shrink–swell droughts share a climate. Modelling that properly makes it 94 times more likely that the same home is hit by two or more perils in one year — 0.11% of years against 0.0012% under independence. But both are rare, so a single home's own 1-in-100 loss barely moves: measured at ±0 within simulation noise. The dependence surfaces where it should, in the aggregate — catastrophic years cost 5.2% more than an independence assumption implies.

×2.4flood cost, typical → bad

Bad years aren't just "more weather"

Weather is the workhorse loss in every year, but it is the secondary perils that turn a typical year (£68 per policy) into a bad one (£134). Going from typical to bad, flood cost multiplies 2.4× and subsidence 2.1×, while weather multiplies just 1.3×.

TVaRnot VaR

The obvious risk measure gives the wrong answer here

With rare compound losses, tail dependence concentrates claims into fewer, worse years — pushing losses beyond the 99.5% quantile. Value-at-Risk can therefore fall as dependence rises. Premiums use expected shortfall (TVaR 99%), which is monotone in dependence and doesn't hide the clustering.

20% vs 33%rating-group churn

Dependence sets the tail; the marginals set the ranking

Perturbing the copula — dependence strength ±25%, conditional correlations zeroed or doubled — moves at most 20% of districts into a different rating group. Perturbing the loss assumptions moves up to 33%. The vine is what makes the tail honest (uplift ranges -4–-5% across scenarios), but if you want the ranking right, spend your effort on claim frequencies and severities. Every scenario is tabulated on the year analysis page.

25%of a home's claims cost

Each peril is pinned to what the market actually paid

Storm, flood and subsidence are calibrated separately to the ABI's 2025 payouts — £244m, £312m and £307m across ~15.5m policies — rather than to a single blended figure. Together they come to £56 per policy per year, about a quarter of what all home claims cost. The rest of a real premium is escape of water, theft, fire, accidental damage and expenses, none of which this models. How the calibration works →

100%of open data

Every hazard input is public, and it matches the record

BGS geology, Met Office climatologies, Environment Agency, Natural Resources Wales and SEPA flood maps, EA groundwater alert areas — all Open Government Licence, all fetched by scripts in the repo. Reconstructing 35 years of those drivers picks out 1990, 2015 and 2020 as the worst storm years and 1995, 2018 and 2022 as the driest summers — the Burns' Day storm, Desmond, Ciara and Dennis, and the known subsidence-surge years. Built on open data

Nineteen datasets from nine public bodies, each fetched by a script in the repository and documented endpoint-by-endpoint — including the quirks that cost hours to discover, and the dead ends so nobody repeats them.

British Geological Survey Met Office Environment Agency Natural Resources Wales SEPA ECMWF ERA5 ONS Census 2021 National Records of Scotland ABI
This is a demonstration model, not a priced product. The hazard data is real and current, and each peril's loss level is pinned to published ABI payouts — but these are four-peril technical premiums, not home insurance premiums. A real policy also covers escape of water, theft, fire and accidental damage, and carries expenses, commission and profit on top. The caveats section is explicit about every assumption.
