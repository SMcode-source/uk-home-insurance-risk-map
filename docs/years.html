How widespread are claims in each kind of year?

Share of districts with at least one claim per peril. The spread between good and catastrophic years is deliberately modest: the spatial loading is calibrated so a 1-in-100 year runs at about twice the claim frequency of an average year, which is already generous against the record. UK weather is strongly correlated across the country, but UK claims are not — a storm crosses every county while only a small, weakly correlated fraction of homes end up claiming.

Does this match real UK years?

The loss model can't be validated without claims data — but its hazard drivers can. These are the real drivers, reconstructed from 35 years of ERA5 reanalysis over twelve UK locations. If the model is watching the right things, the years it would call bad should be the years Britain actually remembers.

Storm days (gusts ≥ 70 km/h) Summer rainfall deficit (subsidence driver)

Storm days and summer deficits are means across the twelve points; the deficit is measured against each point's own 1991–2020 June–August normal. Event names are the historical record, not model output — they are what these years are known for.

Which assumptions matter?

Each row re-runs the simulation (1-in-3 district sample, 8,000 years) with one assumption perturbed. “Group churn” is the share of sampled districts whose rating-group decile changes vs the sampled baseline.

Method: 20,000 simulated portfolio years; perils joined per district by a C-vine copula (weather-root; Gumbel weather–flood, weather–groundwater and weather–subsidence; Gaussian flood–groundwater and flood–subsidence given weather). Each district's year combines the national systemic factor per peril — which carries the vine dependence — with district-level noise (spatial loadings: weather 50%, flood 40%, subsidence 60%, groundwater 70%; storm, drought and aquifer-recharge years are large-scale in the UK, flood more localised), so good years still see scattered claims while bad years cluster nationally. Hazard data: BGS, Met Office, EA/NRW/SEPA (OGL) — see DATA_SOURCES.md. Loss frequencies/severities are illustrative assumptions.
